# Findings — Sales Analysis

## Revenue by month
Revenue shows a clear seasonal pattern that repeats every year: February is consistently the weakest month (as low as $4,810 in Feb 2011), while September, November, and December are consistently the strongest, peaking at $112,326 in November 2014. Revenue also trends upward year-over-year — most months in 2014 outperform the same month in prior years.

## Revenue by quarter
Q4 is the strongest quarter in every single year without exception, and by a wide margin — 2014 Q4 revenue ($280,594) is more than double Q1 of the same year ($118,895). Q1 is consistently the weakest quarter across all four years. Each year's Q4 also outperforms the prior year's Q4, confirming steady year-over-year growth on top of the seasonal cycle.

## Top 5 products by revenue
Revenue leaders are dominated by high-ticket single items rather than high-volume everyday products — the Canon imageCLASS 2200 Advanced Copier leads at $61,599.83, followed by binding machines, a videoconferencing unit, task chairs, and a binding system. This makes sense given revenue is price × quantity: a small number of expensive units can outproduce many small office-supply sales combined.

## Top 5 customers by spend
Sean Miller is the top customer by a clear margin at $25,043.07, ahead of Tamara Chand ($19,052.22), Raymond Buch, Tom Ashbrook, and Adrian Barton. The gap between #1 and #2 suggests Sean Miller may be worth flagging as a key account for retention focus.

## Revenue by region
West leads revenue at $725,457.93, followed closely by East ($678,781.36). Central and South trail meaningfully, with South lowest at $391,721.90 — roughly half of West's total. This regional gap may be worth investigating for expansion or marketing focus in underperforming regions.

## Revenue by category
Technology leads at $836,154.10, narrowly ahead of Furniture ($741,999.98) and Office Supplies ($719,046.99). Unlike the regional split, category revenue is fairly balanced — no single category dominates by a wide margin.

## Month-over-month growth rate
Growth is highly seasonal and cyclical rather than randomly volatile. The sharpest single-month growth was March 2012 at +215.02%, reflecting a rebound after weak Q1 months. The sharpest decline was January 2013 at -75.25%, part of a recurring pattern where revenue consistently craters right after December's peak. This growth/decline swing repeats every year in roughly the same shape.

## Running total of revenue
Cumulative revenue reached $2,297,201.07 by December 2014, the end of the dataset — confirming steady overall growth across the four-year period despite the sharp month-to-month seasonal swings.

## Product ranking by category
The top-ranked product in each category is: HON 5400 Series Task Chairs for Big and Tall (Furniture, $21,870.57), Fellowes PB500 Electric Punch Plastic Comb Binding Machine (Office Supplies, $27,453.38), and Canon imageCLASS 2200 Advanced Copier (Technology, $61,599.83) — the same copier that leads company-wide revenue overall.

## Churn-style analysis (customers inactive the following year)
1,053 customer-year pairs show a customer purchasing in one year but not returning the next. Given the dataset only has ~800 unique customers total, this indicates a substantial share of customers are one-time or infrequent buyers rather than repeat purchasers — a strong signal for prioritizing customer retention efforts.

## Average order value trend
Average order value stayed roughly flat over the four-year period, fluctuating mostly between $350 and $630 with no clear upward or downward trend — in contrast to total revenue, which grew substantially over the same period. This suggests revenue growth is being driven by an increasing number of orders rather than customers spending more per order.

## Discount vs. profit margin (self-authored question)
**Why this matters:** discounting drives sales volume but can silently erode margin — a stakeholder deciding discount policy needs to see this tradeoff directly, not just revenue in isolation.

Tables, Bookcases, and Supplies are all operating at a **net loss** despite generating real revenue. Tables show the worst result: a 26.1% average discount driving a -8.56% profit margin (-$17,725.59 loss on $206,965.68 of sales). Bookcases show a similar pattern at 21.1% average discount and -3.02% margin. Supplies lose money even at a comparatively modest 7.7% average discount, suggesting its problem may be thin margins generally rather than discounting alone. This is a clear, actionable finding: discount policy for Tables and Bookcases specifically should be reviewed, as current discount levels appear to exceed what the margin on these products can sustain.
